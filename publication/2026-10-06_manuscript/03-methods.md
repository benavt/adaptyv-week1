# Computational design and structural assessment of putatively pH-sensitive EGFR binders

Working methods subsection, 2026-10-06. Campaign provenance, construct sequences, additional literature, and unresolved reproducibility details are documented in [the reference library](../2026-10-06_reference-library.md) and [candidate evidence manifest](../../candidates/2026-10-06_readme-panel_001/manifest.json). The subsection describes computational work and its design hypothesis; experimental pH selectivity has not been established in the evidence reviewed here.

## Methods subsection

We pursued acid-favored EGFR binding through proton-linked competition between an EGFR-binding component (DD1) and a tethered masking extension (DD2). The intended mechanism combines acidic DD1 contacts with EGFR histidines and a DD2 interaction that becomes less favorable upon histidine protonation. This follows the coupling between ionization, intraprotein interactions, and ligand binding described by Laskowski and Scheraga [1,2]. Designed histidine networks provide precedents for pH-dependent conformational changes and disassembly [3–5], while recent binder designs use loss of histidine hydrogen-bond acceptance and increased cationic repulsion to weaken interactions at acidic pH [6]. We adapted this latter rationale to a proposed mask: destabilization of DD2–DD1 would expose DD1, rather than intentionally destabilizing the DD1–EGFR interface. The nominal design comparison was pH 6.5 versus 7.4; individual site ionization was treated as environment-dependent.

For the two-Asp DD1 campaign, RFdiffusion3 (RFD3) [7] generated 100 placements of two identity-fixed, coordinate-mobile aspartates around human EGFR D3 His37 and His100 (His346 and His409 in the 310–501 construct). Four placement parents were then scaffolded into 100 binders each, with lengths of 40–100 residues, preserving the Asp heavy-atom geometry and fixed receptor coordinates. Generation used seeds 137–140 for the four parents. Each of the 400 sequences was predicted against human and mouse EGFR D3 using Boltz-2 [8] with seed 101, three recycles, 200 sampling steps, one diffusion sample, cached receptor alignments, empty binder alignments, and no templates. Selection required ipSAE [9] greater than 0.63 and binder TM-score greater than 0.75 in both species; ipSAE used 15 Å PAE/distance cutoffs and the maximum directional score, and TM-score was normalized to the RFD3 binder reference. This retained two pair-11 designs.

The DD2 extension campaign used the selected 77-residue DD1 parent, preserving its sequence and coordinates while generating 1,000 covalent N-terminal extensions of 10–20 residues with RFD3 (seed 139). An ordered Lys–His–Lys motif with variable intervening sequence and free coordinates was imposed, with DD1 Glu2 and Asp46 as hotspots. These DD2 hotspots are distinct from the original DD1 two-Asp motif. Each fusion was evaluated as a monomer and in human- and mouse-EGFR complexes using the Boltz-2 settings above. The five highest monomer pTM designs were submitted for additional conformational sampling. The sequence motif specified residue identities and order; it did not establish a spatial hydrogen-bond network or pH response.

In a parallel interface-redesign branch, ProtonPottsMPNN generated 1,000 variants each of DD1_062, DD1_195, and DD1_310 using protonation-state-aware sequence optimization. Human and mouse complexes were predicted with Boltz-2 (seed 0, three recycles, 200 steps, one sample, target-only templates). Post-prediction scoring enumerated binary protonation states at the recorded design centers and weighted complex-minus-isolated-partner Potts scores by independent Henderson–Hasselbalch probabilities using bound-complex PROPKA pKa estimates [10]. The comparison was pH 6.5 versus 7.3, with a common pKa shift of ±1 used for sensitivity analysis; DD1_062 retained its original 6.5-versus-7.4 generation metadata. These arithmetic averages are model-unit screening proxies, not thermodynamic binding free energies.

For the DD1/DD2 construct assessed here, D46_130_model_1, the 91-residue fusion comprised DD2 residues 1–14 (`AAAKEHTVGEPLAK`) followed by unchanged DD1 residues 15–91. AFSample3 [11], based on AlphaFold 3 [12], sampled this chain with human EGFR D3 and four covalently attached glycans. The run used seeds 1–200, five diffusion samples per seed, ten recycles, an MSA cap of 1,024, `msa_rand_fraction=0.4`, and MSA shuffling, yielding 1,000 recorded models. Binder alignments/templates were empty; EGFR used a sequence-matched cached alignment and a protein-coordinate medoid template. The retained analysis summarized chain-A shared-residue Cα distance matrices using PCA, UMAP, and t-SNE and reported four conformational clusters; the five highest-pTM models per cluster were aligned to the full-cluster Cα RMSD medoid. Neither Boltz-2 nor AFSample3 inference was explicitly pH-conditioned. Their confidence scores and sampled conformations were used for structural assessment, without assigning equilibrium populations, switching kinetics, or experimentally validated pH-dependent affinity [13].
